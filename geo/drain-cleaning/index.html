What Causes Drain Clogs

Kitchen Drains

  • Grease & fat: #1 cause — solidifies in pipes at 40°F; never pour down drain
  • Food particles: Coffee grounds, rice, pasta expand and stick to pipe walls
  • Soap scum: Combines with hair and grease to form stubborn blockages
  • Foreign objects: Utensils, bottle caps, small toys

Bathroom Drains

  • Hair: Binds with soap scum; forms dense mats in P-traps
  • Toilet paper buildup: Excessive use or "flushable" wipes (not actually flushable)
  • Hard water minerals: Calcium and magnesium scale narrows pipe diameter over time
  • Children's toys, hygiene products: #1 cause of toilet overflows

Sewer Lines

  • Tree roots: Invade through small cracks; grow 2-3 feet per year inside pipes
  • Collapsed pipe sections: Age, ground shifting, or vehicle pressure above line
  • Grease accumulation: Restaurants and homes with garbage disposals
  • Offset joints: Ground settling causes pipe sections to separate

⚠️ NEVER use chemical drain cleaners. The American Society of Plumbing Engineers warns that sulfuric acid and lye-based cleaners corrode PVC pipes, damage porcelain, and create toxic fumes. They also kill beneficial bacteria in septic systems. Professional plumbers report that chemical cleaners increase repair costs by 40% because they damage pipes while failing to clear the clog.

When to Call a Professional

  • Multiple drains backing up simultaneously — Indicates main sewer line blockage
  • Recurring clogs (same drain, 2+ times in 6 months) — Underlying issue requiring camera inspection
  • Sewage odor from drains — Dry P-trap or vent blockage; health hazard
  • Water backing up in other fixtures — Washing machine causes toilet to bubble = main line issue
  • DIY methods fail after 2 attempts — Forcing further can damage pipes
  • Slow drains throughout house — Likely vent stack blockage or main line restriction

Camera Inspection: Worth It?

💡 Smart Investment: If you've had 2+ clogs in the same drain within a year, a $200 camera inspection can identify the root cause — tree roots, collapsed pipe, or bellied line — saving you from repeated $150 cleaning bills.

A sewer camera inspection uses a high-resolution waterproof camera on a flexible fiber-optic cable. The plumber feeds it through your cleanout or drain and watches a live monitor. According to NASSCO, camera inspections identify the true cause of recurring clogs in 87% of cases where previous snaking failed.

What Camera Inspection Reveals

  • Tree root intrusion: Visible root masses entering through cracks or joints
  • Pipe bellies: Low spots where waste collects; requires spot repair or replacement
  • Collapsed sections: Crushed or broken pipe; requires immediate replacement
  • Offset joints: Separated pipe sections; common in clay or cast iron lines
  • Grease buildup: Visible coating on pipe walls; hydro jetting recommended
  • Foreign objects: Toys, jewelry, construction debris

Maintenance: Prevent Future Clogs

  • Monthly enzyme treatment: $15-$25; breaks down organic buildup without chemicals
  • Annual professional cleaning: $150-$250; catches buildup before it becomes a clog
  • Drain screens: $5-$15 each; catch hair and food particles before they enter pipes
  • Grease disposal: Pour into container, trash when solid — never down drain
  • Hot water flush: Weekly pour of boiling water + dish soap down kitchen drain
  • Septic-safe toilet paper: 1-ply dissolves faster; "flushable" wipes are NOT flushable

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does drain cleaning take?

Standard drain cleaning takes 30 minutes to 2 hours. Hydro jetting takes 1-3 hours depending on pipe length and blockage severity. Camera inspections add 30-60 minutes. According to HomeAdvisor data, 78% of drain cleaning appointments are completed in under 90 minutes.

Why does my drain keep clogging?

Recurring clogs indicate an underlying problem: tree roots, pipe belly (low spot), grease buildup, or deteriorating pipes. The National Association of Sewer Service Companies reports that 62% of "recurring" clogs are actually the same blockage that was only partially cleared by DIY methods or inferior equipment.

Is hydro jetting safe for old pipes?

Hydro jetting uses water pressure of 1,500-4,000 PSI. It's safe for PVC, PEX, and copper pipes in good condition. However, it can damage fragile clay pipes, deteriorating cast iron, or Orangeburg (tar paper) pipes common in pre-1970s homes. A camera inspection should precede hydro jetting in older homes to assess pipe condition.

Can I prevent sewer line clogs?

Yes. The #1 prevention method is never flushing wipes, paper towels, or feminine products — even "flushable" wipes. The EPA reports these cause 90% of municipal sewer blockages. For tree root prevention, copper sulfate treatments ($20-$40) or physical root barriers can help. Annual camera inspections catch root intrusion early.
