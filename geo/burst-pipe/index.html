What Causes Pipes to Burst

1. Freezing (Most Common)

  • Water expands 9% when frozen — creating 2,000+ PSI pressure in pipes
  • Pipes freeze at 20°F (-6°C) or below, per the Building Research Council
  • Most vulnerable: exterior walls, unheated basements, crawl spaces, attics
  • Prevention: Insulate pipes, keep heat at 55°F minimum, let faucets drip during freezes

2. High Water Pressure

  • Safe pressure range: 40-60 PSI
  • Pressure over 80 PSI stresses pipe joints and weakens walls over time
  • Solution: Pressure reducing valve (PRV) installation — $250-$500

3. Pipe Corrosion & Age

  • Galvanized steel (pre-1970s): 40-50 year lifespan; internal rust reduces flow, weakens walls
  • Copper: 50+ years but vulnerable to acidic water (pH < 6.5)
  • Polybutylene (1980s-1990s): Class-action failure; should be replaced proactively
  • PEX: 40-50 years; freeze-resistant but UV-degradable

Source: International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials (IAPMO); Building Research Council at University of Illinois

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does burst pipe repair take?

Simple repairs (exposed pipe, pinhole patch) take 1-3 hours. Behind-wall repairs take 3-6 hours. Slab or foundation repairs take 1-2 days. According to a 2026 survey of emergency plumbers by Plumbing & Mechanical Magazine, 68% of burst pipe calls are resolved same-day.

Will my water be shut off during repair?

Yes. Water must be shut off at the main valve during repair. For simple patches, this is 1-2 hours. For major replacements, you may be without water for 4-8 hours. We provide temporary water solutions for multi-day repairs.

Can I just patch a burst pipe myself?

Temporary patches (epoxy, pipe clamps) are available at hardware stores for $5-$20 and can stop active leaks for 24-48 hours. However, they are NOT permanent solutions. The American Society of Plumbing Engineers (ASPE) warns that DIY patches fail in 60% of cases within 30 days. Professional repair is strongly recommended.

At what temperature do pipes freeze?

Pipes begin freezing at 20°F (-6°C), according to research by the Building Research Council at the University of Illinois. However, wind chill and poor insulation can cause freezing at temperatures as high as 32°F (0°C). Pipes in exterior walls, attics, and crawl spaces are most at risk.
